# Phase 2 results: vendor-matching prototype

Generated by `src/phase2_evaluation.py`. Every number below comes from that run.

> **Caveat.** Relevance uses the **AI-judged `expected_score`** (Claude Code against the rubric), not human ground truth. The match-score model was trained on the same kind of AI-judged scores.

## 1. Setup

- Test requirements: R09, R10, R16, R18, R24, R28 (the 6 held out of the Phase 1 linear model; it never saw them).
- Each has 15 annotated vendors, 90 pairs in all. Each method re-ranks exactly those 15 (only they have an expected_score).
- Relevant = expected_score ≥ 70: 23 of 90 pairs.
- Eligibility: R1 category and R2 distance; R3 is off because no keyword is required.
- Methods: **pipeline** (eligible first, then predicted score), **nearest** (eligible first, then distance), **random** (one permutation per requirement, seed 42).

## 2. Results (mean over the 6 requirements)

| method | NDCG@5 | precision@5 | relevant vendors excluded by eligibility |
|---|---|---|---|
| pipeline | 0.9601 | 0.7667 | 0 |
| nearest | 0.8741 | 0.7667 | 0 |
| random | 0.1322 | 0.1667 | n/a (no rules) |

For context, random ranking averaged over 1000 permutations gives NDCG@5 0.2970; one seed is noisy.

Precision@5 is capped below 1 when a requirement has fewer than 5 relevant vendors.

**Read with care: the judged pool makes eligibility easy.** The 15 vendors per requirement were sampled in Phase 1 by stratum (5 nearest in category, 4 in category but beyond the radius, 3 nearest other-category, 3 random). Eligible pairs by stratum: near_in_category 30; relevant pairs by stratum: near_in_category 23. So the rules mostly recover the sampling design, and the pipeline-vs-nearest difference comes from ordering within the eligible vendors and from which ineligible vendors come next. Precision@5 is identical for the two because both put the same eligible vendors in the top 5.

## 4. Limitations

- **AI-judged target.** Relevance and the training target are AI-judged scores, not human ground truth; human validation was not done.
- **Noisy category labels.** R1 trusts source-file categories; mislabelled vendors pass R1 and get the category coefficient (see the worst errors).
- **Snapshot dataset.** One 2026-09-27 snapshot; no opening hours, stock or prices.
- **Clipped scores.** Displayed scores are clipped to 0-100; raw values are kept.
- **Category check only for pharmacy.** Other categories have no classifier.
- **Small evaluation.** 6 requirements × 15 judged vendors; the full candidate lists the app shows (up to hundreds of vendors) are mostly unjudged.
